Maximise your votes by splitting them

Two Votes,
Two MPs.

Split your votes. Party vote NZ First or ACT. Electorate National. That is how one ballot helps put two Coalition MPs in the House. A National party vote is a wasted vote.

Party vote

NZ First or ACT Whichever partner you prefer. The page does not pick. Do not waste this on National, or on a protest vote for a non-parliamentary party.

Electorate vote

National The candidate most likely to win the seat for the Coalition — except Epsom and Tāmaki, which stay ACT.
Why this works Your electorate

If you party-vote National, you waste that vote.

It elects no extra Coalition MP. Maximise your votes by splitting them: party-vote NZ First or ACT. A protest vote for a non-parliamentary party elects nobody either.

ZERO LIST SEATS for National is the point

On the latest Taxpayers’ Union–Curia poll National is entitled to 40 of the 120. On the 2026 notional map it holds 45 electorates. If it holds those seats it takes no list MPs and a five-seat overhang. Extra National party votes do not grow the Coalition. They just refill a quota the electorates have already filled. That is a wasted vote.

Maximise your votes by splitting them. Move the party vote to a partner who is still taking list seats. Parliament grows only if National’s electorate haul exceeds its party-vote entitlement — an overhang. Other parties keep their share of the 120.

Party-vote National and you waste it. A protest vote for a non-parliamentary party is the same dead end: nobody new gets in. Party-vote NZ First or ACT to keep the left out.
