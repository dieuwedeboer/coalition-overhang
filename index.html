How your two votes work

MMP gives you two votes. They do different jobs. These are the rules of the system.

  • Electorate vote — picks your local MP.
  • Party vote — sets each qualifying party’s share of the 120. List MPs fill any gap.
  • Overhang — win more electorates than that share and you keep every seat, take no list MPs, and Parliament grows. Nobody else’s 120 allocation is cut.
  • On current polling National is likely to get zero list seats — this allows you to split your two votes and generate an overhang.
  • Te Pāti Māori is officially running this vote splitting tactic on the left — electorate vote only, party vote for Labour/Green. This page explains it for Coalition voters.

On current polling, National takes zero list seats.

A National party vote elects no extra Coalition MP. The electorates have already filled its share. Maximise your votes by splitting them: party-vote NZ First or ACT.

Zero list seats, on current polling

On the latest Taxpayers’ Union–Curia poll National is entitled to 40 of the 120. It currently holds 44 electorates. Hold those seats and National takes no list MPs — a four-seat overhang. Extra National party votes refill a filled quota. They do not add a Coalition MP.

Move the party vote to a partner still taking list seats. That is how one ballot helps put two Coalition MPs in the House.

On current polling a National party vote elects zero MPs. A protest vote for a non-parliamentary party is the same. Party-vote NZ First or ACT to keep the left out.

What your electorate vote does

These are 2023 candidate votes, not percentages. The National seats with the smallest winning margin are at risk — lose those and there is no overhang. Winnable seats follow. Then every other seat by how close National is, smallest margin first.
